1 a.m., woken by another thud. You want to tell the building manager, but you have nothing that shows when it happened or how loud it was. Recording all night means listening back to hours of audio. And while you are out, you have no way of knowing what went on.

Floor Noise Log throws away the quiet minutes and keeps only the ones with loud sounds. When you wake up or get home, you can see what time it happened, how many decibels, and how many times.

02 / 06 · Events

Only sounds above the threshold
become events

Sounds above the threshold (50 dB by default, adjustable from 40 to 80 dB) are marked as events. One-minute files with no events are not saved, so storage lasts longer. Turn on record on event and recording starts 5 seconds before the sound.

Three steps

  1. Install and start measuring

    Allow the microphone and notifications, then tap the play button in the middle. Place the phone on the floor or a desk where it can pick up the sound.

  2. Leave it on when you sleep or go out

    Measuring continues with the screen off. On Android, exclude the app from battery optimization for long sessions. On iPhone, turn on Background Recording in Settings.

  3. Check when you wake up or get home

    Only records with events are kept, sorted by date. Listen again, add notes and add them to your log.

Readings are for everyday reference. Phone microphones differ in sensitivity, so readings carry some error and do not replace an official measurement. Use mic calibration (−20 to +20 dB) in Settings to adjust for your device.
